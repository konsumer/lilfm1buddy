lilfm1buddy

Switch the M-VAVE FM-1's firmware from the FM-1 itself: a tiny ESP32-S3 on its USB port, a menu on its screen, no computer.

The little sibling of fm1buddy.

Install Drive Use it Case

You need

  • An ESP32-S3 board with PSRAM. Recommended: the Seeed XIAO ESP32S3 Plus: 21 × 18 mm, room for about 20 firmwares, a battery charger on board, and it reads its battery (the FM-1 shows its level). Its LED blinks what it's doing. Much cheaper boards work too, with room for fewer firmwares: the Seeed XIAO ESP32S3 (8 MB, about 10 firmwares), the Waveshare ESP32-S3-Zero (4 MB, about 5 firmwares, or 3 backups), or any ESP32-S3 DevKit N16R8 (16 MB).
  • Optional: a 3.7 V 180 mAh LiPo with bare wires, so it runs on its own battery. Without one, it works great powered over USB: a USB splitter (one side to the FM-1, the other to power) lets you run it off an ordinary phone power bank. To add one, solder it to the BAT+ and BAT− pads on the XIAO's back. Check the polarity with a meter first: the wire colours on these aren't always right. A firmware switch takes a few mAh, and the XIAO charges it from its USB-C (its red LED flashes while it does, and goes out when it's full).
  • Chrome or Edge on a computer, and a USB-C cable that carries data

No power switch: when it's done, or nothing happens for a while, the board goes to deep sleep (months on a battery). Press its BOOT button (or reset) to wake it.

Already set up? You don't need to install again: the program on the board doesn't change. An update is just the files on its drive, the menus (lilfm1.bin, lilsmk.bin): get them on the Drive tab.

1. The program

Pick your board, and plug it into this computer. A new board: hold its BOOT button, tap reset, and let go (its download mode). One with lilfm1buddy on it: if its LILBUDDY drive shows up on this computer, eject it first (its LED goes blue: it's ready to install). Then press Install. A new board, or one with lilfm1buddy 0.1 on it: let it erase. To update one, don't: its files stay. Then tap reset.

Erasing deletes everything on its drive, backups too: copy them to your computer first.

Your browser can't: use Chrome or Edge on a computer. This page has to be opened over https.

No build yet: the page has no firmware next to it.

2. Firmwares

On a computer the board is a USB drive, LILBUDDY (its LED cyan). Put .fwsc firmwares on it (drag them there, or from the Drive tab). Eject it when you're done.

The board carries its own loader for the FM-1's flash, and draws the flash's sectors on the FM-1's screen as it writes or reads: there's nothing to put on its drive for that.

The case

          
Three printed pieces, snap-fit, no screws: the base, the lid, and the BOOT button's own insert (so the button is a separate colour, and prints without supports). The lid clicks onto the walls and its ribs hold the board down on its shelf; RESET is a paperclip hole.

          
The BOOT and RESET switches are where the board's maker put them, and the starting numbers here are measured guesses: print the check plate first (about a minute), lay it on the board, and move its holes until they sit over the two switches. Then make the case.

  2. With a midimap.txt on its drive, it routes MIDI between them (make one on fm1buddy's MIDI map page; a line bluetooth adds Bluetooth MIDI). Without one, it waits.

  3. Press BOOT for the menu on the FM-1's screen. With stock V15 or a Felucca-based firmware running, the board sends the FM-1 into its boot loader by itself. With any other (SLOOP, ...), or none, the LED says switch it off, then on: do (the FM-1 straight on the board for this, no hub).
    4. 
            
  4. The FM-1 shows the list: Back up this FM-1, then the firmwares and backups. Turn SEL to move, press OCT+ to pick, and OCT+ again to be sure (anything else goes back).

  5. The board writes it (or backs it up to fm1-N.bin) and the FM-1 shows its flash as it goes; then the FM-1 starts its new firmware, and the board restarts too, ready for the next one.

The XIAO's LED is one colour: the same blinks, without the colours.

          
Until something is picked nothing is written: to keep what's on it, switch the FM-1 off and on. Only the firmware's own image is written, never the flash's head (what the chip starts from), and the chip's boot loader is in its ROM: nothing here can make it unrecoverable.
