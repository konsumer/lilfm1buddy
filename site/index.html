lilfm1buddy

Switch the M-VAVE FM-1's firmware from the FM-1 itself: a tiny ESP32-S3 on its USB port, a menu on its screen, no computer.

Install Firmwares Use it

You need

An ESP32-S3 board (pick yours, the install button follows):

  • a battery: the board is the FM-1's USB host, each on its own battery, one USB-C data cable between them (no hub, no charger in between). A single-cell (1S, 3.7 V) LiPo, 150 to 500 mAh: a few mAh a firmware switch, and the XIAO charges it at about 100 mA from its USB-C (its red charge LED flashes while it does, and goes out when it's full). Small ones made for toy helicopters (like the Syma S107's) do, but often have no protection circuit: take one that says it has, or let the board's sleep (below) look after it. On the XIAO, cut off the plug and solder the leads to the BAT+ and BAT− pads on its back. Check the polarity with a meter first: the wires on these packs aren't always where their plug says.
  • no power switch: when it's done (or nothing happens for a while) the board goes to deep sleep, a few tens of µA (months on a small battery). Press its reset button to wake it for the next switch.
  • Chrome or Edge on a computer, and a USB-C cable that carries data

1. The program

Plug the board into this computer and press Install. A new board: let it erase. To update one, don't: its firmwares stay.

Your browser can't: use Chrome or Edge on a computer. This page has to be opened over https.

No build yet: the page has no firmware next to it.

2. JieLi's loader, and firmwares

The board writes the FM-1's flash with the loader JieLi made for its chip (wl82loader.bin). It isn't ours to hand out: this page gets it from jl-uboot-tool, checks it, and puts it on the board. Then add the firmwares you want on it.

Until a firmware is picked nothing is written: to keep what's on it, switch the FM-1 off and on. Only the firmware's own image is written, never the flash's head (what the chip starts from), and the chip's boot loader is in its ROM: nothing here can make it unrecoverable.
